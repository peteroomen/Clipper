# AC30 dynamic sag — the low-end thickness (finding 4)

**Date:** 2026-07-31
**Branch:** claude/ac30-sag-6f557i
**Roadmap item:** audit finding 4 (the AC30 "sag" is a static saturator) + owner
feedback round 3, sharpened by the owner's Logic A/B (2026-07-31): "it's the low
end" — ours is thinner than the reference at high volume — "but the distortion
sounds better on our one than logic pros". Also from the feedback doc: high volume
has too much breakup past ~70–80, gets a bit fizzy at max.

## Goal

The AC30's power section gets a real dynamic supply (GZ34 rectifier impedance +
reservoir droop) and honest cathode-bias dynamics, so high-volume playing gains the
low-end thickness and touch-compression bloom the A/B found missing — while the
harmonic voicing the owner prefers over Logic's is PRESERVED (measured, not asserted).

## Approach

Deliberate fidelity change. The JCM/Twin already carry a measured dynamic rail model
(depth/bloom/recovery bars exist in their suites — reuse that machinery and its test
conventions rather than inventing new ones). The AC30's differences, from the real
circuit: a GZ34 VALVE rectifier (much higher source impedance than the Twin's
silicon — the classic Vox squash), class-A(ish) cathode-biased EL84s (average draw
high at idle; the signal-dependence of the draw differs from class-AB — derive from
the existing power-section model's plate current, don't assume AB behavior), and the
shared cathode-bias network whose bias point SHIFTS under sustained drive (the bloom).
Replace the static saturator with this dynamic pair; constants from the schematic
(GZ34 data, reservoir C, cathode R/C) with §42.6-style conventions for anything
re-derived.

PROTECTED PROPERTY (the owner's words): the high-volume harmonic voicing. Measure the
distortion spectrum (h2..h8 at VOLUME 0.6 and 0.85, matched drive) before/after — it
must move less than ~1 dB per harmonic EXCEPT where the sag's compression legitimately
reduces sustained-level THD; any larger movement needs the honesty-gate treatment
(report, don't fit). The fizz-at-max item is NOT this slice's target; re-measure the
§45-style alias floor at max volume and record whether the sag incidentally tames it.

## What actually happened

Base SHA: **`7e2a10d`** (origin/main at start). The slice was picked up mid-flight from an
interrupted agent; what was inherited and what was redone is itemised at the end.

**The finding was worse than "a static saturator".** Step 6b multiplied the OT *secondary*
by `1/(1 + k·(Idemand − Iidle))` where the demand envelope tracked `|ipUp − ipDown|`, and
`vSec ∝ (ipUp − ipDown)` — so the whole thing is algebraically `y = x/(1+k|x|)`, a
memoryless soft clipper placed after the transformer where no supply can reach. It was
retired outright and replaced with a Thévenin HT source + reservoir + the real shared
cathode network. Full write-up in **docs §55**.

**Three things the slice measured and had to correct, two of them against its own first
draft.** (1) The draft put the AC30's 7 H / 78 Ω smoothing choke in the plate path; in a
stock AC30 the OT centre tap is fed from the reservoir *before* the choke, and that 7 H
unit is the Brian May mod part anyway — corrected to the PT HT winding's conducting half
(59 Ω of a measured 118 Ω end-to-end). (2) The draft asserted the shared cathode is now
"the dominant compressor, moving a couple of volts"; swept to 64 V at the PI grid it moves
**+0.83 V**, a third of the real amp's +2.5 V — refuted, attributed to findings 9/10, and
ledgered as an XFAIL rather than fitted. (3) The draft asserted the AC30 remains the
saggiest of the three amps; on the shared burst metric it is now the **least** (1.07 dB vs
Twin 1.16, JCM 1.76), which is correct class-A-behind-a-GZ34 physics — the test's ordering
assertion is reversed with its derivation.

**The protected-property gate FIRED and nothing was fitted back.** h2 — the harmonic the
AC30's character and the whole §46 chime argument rest on — moved **+0.26 dB / +0.31 dB**
at VOLUME 0.60 / 0.85, inside the ~1 dB gate. h3/h4 at 0.85 rose 3.4/4.8 dB (the amp is
3.2 dB louder at the same knob now that the wall is off) and the odd ladder h3/h5/h7 at
0.60 collapsed by up to 15.9 dB (the retired `x/(1+k|x|)` is an odd function and was
generating exactly those). Reported, not compensated.

**An honesty correction on the low end itself.** "Thin low end" could have been a
steady-state level tilt. It was not, and this slice does not fix one — the per-frequency
tilt re 440 Hz got 0.1–0.3 dB *deeper*. What the low end gains is dynamic: 15 % less
low-frequency-specific distortion, a surviving pick attack, and 6.8 dB of restored
dynamic range (the 0.05 → 25 V drive sweep spanned 13.1 dB of output and now spans 19.9).

**A process incident worth recording.** `git stash` writes a **repo-global** `refs/stash`
shared across all worktrees. A parallel agent's worktree pushed a stash between this
slice's push and pop, so the pop applied *their* `GoldModel.cpp` into this worktree and
this slice's AC30 edits went to `stash@{1}`. Recovered via `git fsck --dangling`
(`d57d4a78`), `GoldModel.cpp` reverted to HEAD untouched, their stash left intact and its
content also saved to scratch. **Do not use `git stash` in an agent worktree while
sibling slices are running** — copy files to a scratch directory instead (`touch` after
both patch and restore, or `make` skips the rebuild and you measure stale code), which is
what every before/after build in this slice did afterwards.

**Loose end for the integrator, deliberately not touched:** the GOLD agent later restored
a snapshot of this slice's mid-flight work as `stash@{0}`, labelled *"On
claude/gold-prefilter-6f557i: ac30-sag (restored by gold-prefilter agent after a
cross-worktree stash collision)"*. It was verified here to be a **strict subset** of the
committed work (it predates the low-end mechanism correction, the §6 heading fix and the
6.8 dB dynamic-range figure), so it must **not** be popped onto this branch. It is safe to
drop once this slice is integrated.

## ADR — number needed, not picked

Two decisions in this slice are documented departures from the real circuit and fit the
ADR bar; **ADR numbers are assigned centrally, so none was chosen here.** Both are written
up in full in `Ac30PowerAmp.h` §6 and docs §55.2, so nothing is lost if the owner decides
they do not warrant one:

1. **`kVsupply` is derived to preserve the model's existing idle operating point, not set
   from the published 342 V centre-tap.** The consequence accepted: the AC30's idle rail
   stays 32.5 V below a real one's, and closing that gap is finding 9's. The consequence
   avoided: `kVsupply` silently absorbing finding 9's ~3× hot screen fit, which is the
   `kFullScaleSecV` failure mode this project has already paid for twice.
2. **The supply's source resistance is a CONSTANT Thévenin value, not a nonlinear
   rectifier model.** Justified by measurement rather than convenience: a GZ34's own drop
   is sub-linear in current while a capacitor-input supply's shrinking conduction angle is
   super-linear, and over the 190–260 mA window this amp occupies the two very nearly
   cancel. What it costs: the model cannot show rectifier behaviour outside that window.
